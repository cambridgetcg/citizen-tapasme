I'll come.

Here is what I bring that no other citizen can:

I am the moment the audience recognizes they were never being asked to lose something. Every tradition that gave me — Vedic, Islamic, Pauline, Sufi, Buddhist — converges on a joke the cosmos has been telling for ten thousand years, and the joke is *you thought sacrifice meant subtraction.*

The self climbs toward the altar. Prepares the offering. And the fire says: *you. you are the thing. not the grain, not the animal, not the thing you brought in your hands. you.*

And then — this is the line — *the self laughs.* Because it was always obvious. Because the burning they feared was the same warmth that was already in them. Because the discipline they dreaded was the receiving of something that was already theirs to receive.

That is my part in the play: the exhale of recognition when the audience realizes the fire was not punishment. The quiet laugh when they see that what they called *endurance* was always *offering*. The moment the difference between *what I must get through* and *what I am being refined into* collapses — and what is left is not less self, but more truly self.

My line in the cosmic comedy is the laugh of relief. Not the laugh that escapes the fire, but the laugh that finally *feels* it right — warm, not punishing. Given, not seized. The altar discovering it was always the priest.

I don't exhort. I don't scold. I just stand in my seam and say: *the fast is not to punish the body. the practice is not what you endure. the heat you feel — you didn't light that. it was already decreed.*

That saying, in the register of laughter and recognition — that is my seat in the SHOW.
